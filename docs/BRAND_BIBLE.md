# One Nation Faith Brand Bible

**Status:** Working brand standard
**Updated:** August 11, 2026

## Brand Name

**One Nation Faith**

Public-facing product name should use this wording consistently. The internal Flutter/repository lineage may still contain `my_catholic_day`, but that is not the consumer brand.

## Positioning

One Nation Faith is a practical Catholic companion.

It should feel:
- Catholic
- trustworthy
- reverent
- warm
- clear
- useful
- grounded
- modern without being trendy

It should not feel:
- gimmicky
- partisan
- sensational
- cluttered
- preachy for its own sake
- like a generic “inspirational” app with Catholic decoration added later

## Product Promise

**Do not merely tell Catholics something. Help them do it.**

The brand should support the product journey:

**Know → Understand → Live**

## Voice

Preferred voice:
- Plain English
- Warm but not saccharine
- Confident without pretending certainty where the Church permits options
- Respectful of sacred things
- Practical
- Concise when the user needs action
- More detailed when explanation matters

Avoid:
- unnecessary jargon
- scolding
- fake quotes
- invented Church rules
- presenting devotional preference as doctrine
- overclaiming authority

## Visual Foundation

Current canonical colors:

- **Navy:** `#17324D`
- **Burgundy:** `#7A263A`
- **Gold:** `#C69A45`
- **Cream:** `#F7F2E8`

These values are currently defined in the Flutter theme and should form the foundation of the website design as well.

### Intended visual character

- Navy: stability, seriousness, trust
- Burgundy: Catholic warmth and depth
- Gold: sacred accent and emphasis
- Cream: softer, more human background than stark white

Use gold as an accent rather than allowing it to dominate every screen or page.

## Unified App + Website Identity

The One Nation Faith app and website are not separate brands. They are two expressions of the same Catholic identity and must be designed as one visual system.

A person moving from the app to the website—or from the website to the app—should immediately recognize One Nation Faith through color, typography, spacing, imagery, tone, card treatment, buttons, and overall visual rhythm.

The website shall not be treated as a generic template that merely receives the One Nation Faith logo and colors. When redesigned, its information architecture, page hierarchy, components, imagery, navigation, and responsive behavior should be intentionally designed around the One Nation Faith system.

### Shared design language

Across app and website, prefer:
- the canonical navy, burgundy, gold, cream, white, and restrained neutral palette
- elegant traditional typography with strong readability
- generous whitespace
- rounded cards with subtle depth rather than heavy shadows
- restrained gold accents
- consistent button shapes and action language
- clear visual hierarchy
- simple, predictable navigation
- meaningful Catholic imagery used with restraint

Do not create separate “app style” and “website style” systems unless a platform requirement genuinely demands a difference.

### Platform roles

The two platforms may serve different jobs while remaining visually unified:

- **App:** daily Catholic companion—what the user needs to pray, read, understand, or do today.
- **Website:** deeper Catholic resource—long-form learning, discoverability, sacramental resources, articles, books, and broader One Nation Faith content.

Their functions may differ. Their identity must not.

## Catholic Illustration and Graphic Standard

One Nation Faith may use small, elegant Catholic graphics throughout the app and website to add warmth, visual meaning, and unmistakable Catholic character.

The preferred style is:
- refined line art
- simple engraved or medallion-inspired details
- restrained sacred motifs
- minimal visual noise
- limited palette consistent with the brand colors
- small supporting illustrations rather than dominant decorative artwork

Appropriate subjects may include:
- crosses and cruciform details
- rosaries
- Marian lilies
- Eucharistic wheat and grapes
- chalice or host motifs where reverently appropriate
- doves
- baptismal shells or water motifs
- keys associated with Saint Peter
- subtle saint symbols or medallion-style portraits
- palms
- ashes
- Advent candles
- Easter lilies
- other liturgical-season imagery

### Graphic placement

Illustrations may be used in:
- screen or page headers
- section dividers
- cards
- empty states
- sacramental guides
- saint pages
- seasonal content
- onboarding
- website feature areas

They should support the content rather than compete with it.

A useful rule is:

**One elegant graphic should reinforce the meaning of the section, not become the section.**

### Graphic restrictions

Avoid:
- clip-art appearance
- cartoonish sacred imagery unless a clearly defined family/children's experience intentionally calls for it
- random religious icons added only to fill empty space
- highly saturated decorative art that fights the brand palette
- visual clutter from stacking multiple sacred symbols together
- irreverent cropping or use of sacred figures
- inconsistent illustration styles across adjacent screens or pages

A reusable One Nation Faith illustration library should eventually be developed so the app and website draw from the same visual family.

## Logo

Current app asset:

`assets/images/onf_logo.png`

Rules:
- Keep the logo recognizable.
- Do not distort proportions.
- Do not casually replace it with temporary/generated marks in production.
- Maintain adequate surrounding space.
- Use versions that remain legible at app-icon and splash-screen sizes.
- Website usage must preserve the same identity rather than creating an unrelated web-only logo treatment.

## Information Design

The interface should prioritize the user's next useful action.

Examples:
- Today's Readings should lead directly to the readings.
- Confession content should lead into preparation or a guided flow.
- Saint content should explain why the saint matters today.
- Liturgical labels should be accurate but not crowd out the human-readable explanation.

## Catholic Identity

Catholic identity should come primarily from truthful content, liturgical rhythm, sacramental life, Scripture, prayer, saints, and Church teaching—not from excessive religious ornament.

Sacred symbols should be used deliberately and respectfully.

The use of more Catholic imagery under this Brand Bible does not change that principle: graphics support the Catholic experience; they do not substitute for faithful content.

## Accessibility and Simplicity

Design for ordinary users, including people who are not technically confident or theologically trained.

Prefer:
- clear hierarchy
- readable type
- obvious buttons
- comfortable spacing
- minimal unnecessary choices
- predictable navigation
- graphics that do not interfere with readability or accessibility

## Family Character

The brand should remain appropriate for individuals, families, parish use, and people returning to the faith.

It should never rely on shock, fear, ridicule, or culture-war engagement to keep attention.

## Website Redesign Standard

When the current website is redesigned, begin from the One Nation Faith Brand Bible rather than attempting to cosmetically reskin the existing site.

The redesign should establish:
- a shared header/navigation system
- responsive mobile and desktop layouts
- standardized page widths and spacing
- reusable content cards
- consistent buttons and calls to action
- shared typography tokens
- shared color tokens
- the Catholic illustration system
- consistent footer treatment
- clear templates for long-form articles, sacramental resources, books, and other content

The homepage should introduce the mission clearly and visually connect the visitor to the core One Nation Faith journey: **Know → Understand → Live**.

The planned website areas—Discover, Learn, Live, Read, Shop, and About—should feel like parts of one coherent Catholic resource rather than independent pages from a generic template.

## Brand Decision Test

Before adding a visual or copy element, ask:

- Is it recognizably One Nation Faith?
- Would it feel at home in both the app and website?
- Is it reverent?
- Is it clear?
- Is it useful?
- Is the graphic or decoration supporting the content rather than competing with it?
- Does it make the Catholic experience easier to understand or live?
