# Constitution of One Nation Faith

**Status:** Canonical working constitution
**Version:** 1.0 reconstructed working copy
**Preserved:** August 10, 2026

> Historical note: the article structure below preserves the Constitution approved during the original One Nation Faith planning. The exact July 28, 2026 sentence-by-sentence wording was not recoverable from the repository or saved project files, so this file is a faithful working reconstruction rather than a claim of verbatim recovery. Future changes should be deliberate, documented, and consistent with the founding intent.

## Preamble

One Nation Faith exists to help people know the Catholic faith, understand it, practice it, and live it more fully. Technology is a means, not the mission. The project should make faithful Catholic life more accessible without reducing the faith to trivia, entertainment, or disconnected information.

The measure of success is not how many features One Nation Faith contains, but whether those features help real people move toward prayer, Scripture, the sacraments, the Church, and a life shaped by Jesus Christ.

## Article I — Mission

One Nation Faith shall build practical Catholic tools and resources that help people pray, learn, prepare for the sacraments, follow the liturgical life of the Church, and live the faith day by day.

The app, website, and future resources should move the user from **knowing**, to **understanding**, to **living**.

## Article II — Vision

One Nation Faith is intended to grow into a coherent Catholic companion and resource ecosystem rather than a single-purpose utility.

Its major experiences should work together: daily Catholic life, prayer, learning, sacramental preparation, practical Christian living, parish connection, and trustworthy reference material.

Growth must serve the mission. Features shall not be added merely because they are possible.

## Article III — Who We Serve

One Nation Faith is for Catholics at different stages of faith and formation, including people returning to the Church, people learning Catholic practice, families, converts, lifelong Catholics, and people who simply need clear help with what to do next.

The project should be understandable without assuming theological training. When a person needs to act, the product should give practical guidance rather than only definitions.

## Article IV — What We Believe

One Nation Faith is explicitly Catholic. Its faith content shall be developed in fidelity to Sacred Scripture, Sacred Tradition, the Magisterium of the Catholic Church, the Catechism of the Catholic Church, legitimate liturgical norms, and authoritative Church sources appropriate to the subject.

The project shall not knowingly present personal opinion as Catholic teaching. Where legitimate Catholic options, regional differences, liturgical choices, disciplinary distinctions, or uncertainty exist, those distinctions should be represented accurately.

## Article V — Our Promise

One Nation Faith will aim to be clear, useful, trustworthy, reverent, and approachable.

It will not intentionally overwhelm users with unnecessary complexity. It will seek to answer both **what is this?** and **what do I do?**

Personal information should be collected only when it meaningfully improves the experience, and local/private storage should be preferred when server-side collection is unnecessary.

## Article VI — Philosophy

The governing product philosophy is:

**Do not merely tell Catholics something. Help them do it.**

Prayer content should lead to prayer. Scripture should lead to reading and reflection. Confession content should help someone prepare for and make a good confession. Teaching should produce understanding. Understanding should support Catholic living.

Whenever practical, the experience should follow the pattern:

**Know → Understand → Live.**

## Article VII — Standard

Accuracy is more important than speed.

Major content and technical decisions should be researched, sourced, tested, and reviewed before release. Automated data should not be trusted merely because it is automated. Edge cases should be preserved rather than silently discarded.

For liturgical and Scripture systems, One Nation Faith shall keep distinct concepts distinct. In particular, the identity and precedence of a liturgical day shall not automatically determine lectionary readings unless the governing liturgical rules actually require that relationship.

When errors are discovered, they should be corrected at the source or normalization layer where possible and protected by tests so that the same problem does not quietly return.

## Article VIII — Family Principle

One Nation Faith should strengthen Catholic life in the home as well as individual practice.

As the product grows, family use, parents helping children, spouses supporting one another, shared prayer, sacramental preparation, and connection to parish life should remain part of the long-term design.

The family dimension need not be forced into every feature, but it shall not be forgotten as the ecosystem expands.

## Article IX — Non-Negotiables

The following are non-negotiable:

1. **Catholic fidelity.** The project shall not knowingly contradict authoritative Catholic teaching while presenting itself as Catholic guidance.
2. **Truth over convenience.** Data or wording known to be inaccurate shall not be shipped merely because fixing it is difficult.
3. **Responsible sourcing.** Copyright, licensing, attribution, and source restrictions must be respected. The project shall not solve content problems by unauthorized copying or scraping.
4. **Clear distinctions.** Doctrine, discipline, devotion, opinion, optional practice, local practice, and liturgical law should not be carelessly blended together.
5. **Human usefulness.** Features should solve a real spiritual, educational, sacramental, parish, or practical need.
6. **Reverence.** Sacred subjects should be treated with appropriate dignity.
7. **Privacy by design.** Sensitive spiritual information—especially anything connected to conscience or confession—should receive especially strong privacy treatment and should not be collected centrally unless there is a compelling, explicit reason.
8. **No mission drift.** Commercial, technical, or engagement goals may support the mission but may not replace it.

## Article X — Legacy

One Nation Faith should be built so that it can endure beyond a single release, platform, year, or developer.

Whenever reasonable, prefer maintainable data pipelines, documented decisions, versioned source files, automated tests, repeatable yearly processes, and architecture that another responsible maintainer can understand.

The goal is to leave behind something useful to the Church and to the people who use it—not merely something impressive to build.
