# Constitution of One Nation Faith

**Status:** Ratified Constitution
**Version:** 1.0
**Ratified:** August 11, 2026

## Preamble

One Nation Faith exists to help people throughout our nation know the Catholic faith, understand it, practice it, and live it more fully. It seeks to strengthen the bonds of faith that unite Catholics across homes, parishes, dioceses, regions, and communities while recognizing that this unity is ultimately rooted not in nationality, politics, or culture, but in Jesus Christ and His Church.

Technology is a means, not the mission. One Nation Faith should make faithful Catholic life more accessible and help people discover that they are not practicing the faith alone, but as members of one Catholic Church sharing one faith.

The measure of success is not how many features One Nation Faith contains, but whether those features help real people move toward prayer, Scripture, the sacraments, the Church, one another, and a life shaped by Jesus Christ.

## Article I — Mission

One Nation Faith shall build practical Catholic tools and resources that help people know the Catholic faith, understand it, pray it, receive and prepare for the sacraments, participate in the liturgical life of the Church, and live the faith day by day.

The project shall begin by serving Catholics throughout the United States, helping users connect the universal Catholic faith with the particular Catholic life of their homes, parishes, dioceses, regions, and communities.

One Nation Faith shall seek to strengthen unity among Catholics without erasing legitimate local differences. Geography, culture, parish life, and regional practice may differ, but the Catholic faith that binds the Church together is one.

Every major experience should help move the user from **Know → Understand → Live**.

## Article II — Vision

One Nation Faith shall seek to become a trusted Catholic companion for everyday life: a place where people can turn to pray, understand the faith, prepare for the sacraments, follow the Church’s liturgical life, connect with their parish and diocese, and discover practical ways to live as Catholics.

It shall begin with the Catholic faithful of the United States while remaining consciously part of the universal Church. One Nation Faith should help Catholics see the connection between the faith celebrated throughout the whole Church and the particular Catholic life of their own state, diocese, parish, family, and community.

The app, website, and future resources should form one coherent Catholic ecosystem rather than a collection of disconnected products. Daily Catholic life, prayer, learning, sacramental preparation, Scripture, parish connection, family life, and trustworthy reference material should reinforce one another.

One Nation Faith may grow in reach, technology, and capability, but growth must always serve the mission. A feature, product, partnership, or expansion shall not be pursued merely because it is popular, profitable, technically possible, or likely to increase engagement.

The long-term vision is not to keep people inside an app. It is to help them live more fully **outside the app**: in prayer, in their homes, in their parishes, in service to others, and in communion with the Catholic Church.

## Article III — Who We Serve

One Nation Faith is built first for ordinary people trying to live, rediscover, learn, or understand the Catholic faith.

It shall serve lifelong Catholics, Catholics returning after time away, converts and those preparing to enter the Church, families, parents helping children, young adults, people beginning to pray seriously, and Catholics who simply need clear guidance about what the Church teaches or what to do next.

It should also welcome people who are not Catholic but sincerely want to understand Catholic belief and practice. They should be able to encounter the faith clearly and respectfully without Catholic teaching being diluted, disguised, or altered merely to make it more acceptable.

One Nation Faith should be understandable without requiring theological education. When specialized Catholic or theological language is necessary, it should be explained rather than simply avoided.

The product should especially remember the person who is standing at the church door, preparing for confession after many years, opening a Bible for the first time, trying to pray, helping a child understand the faith, or wondering, **“What am I supposed to do?”**

Clergy, catechists, teachers, and experienced Catholics may also find One Nation Faith useful, but the product should not become so technical that ordinary Catholics can no longer use it confidently.

One Nation Faith shall never treat a user’s lack of knowledge as a reason for embarrassment. Clear guidance should invite people deeper into the faith while preserving the seriousness and truth of what the Church teaches.

## Article IV — Catholic Identity and Authority

One Nation Faith is explicitly and unapologetically Catholic. Its purpose is not to create a new interpretation of Christianity, but to faithfully help people understand and live the faith entrusted to the Catholic Church.

Catholic teaching presented by One Nation Faith shall be developed in fidelity to **Sacred Scripture, Sacred Tradition, and the Magisterium of the Catholic Church**, with appropriate use of the Catechism of the Catholic Church, canon law, approved liturgical texts and norms, documents of the Holy See, episcopal conferences, diocesan authorities, and other authoritative Catholic sources according to the subject being addressed.

When authoritative sources differ in weight or purpose, One Nation Faith shall respect those distinctions. A dogma, doctrine, disciplinary rule, pastoral judgment, theological opinion, private revelation, devotion, local custom, and personal recommendation shall not be presented as though they carry the same authority.

One Nation Faith shall not knowingly present the opinion of its developers, writers, artificial intelligence systems, contributors, clergy, commentators, or users as though that opinion were the teaching of the Catholic Church.

Artificial intelligence and other technologies may assist with research, organization, explanation, and development, but they shall **never constitute the authority for Catholic teaching**. When an AI-generated explanation conflicts with an authoritative Church source, the authoritative source governs.

Where the Church permits legitimate differences—such as liturgical options, theological schools, devotional practices, disciplinary variations, regional norms, or matters on which the Church has not definitively spoken—One Nation Faith should represent those distinctions fairly rather than manufacture certainty that does not exist.

When One Nation Faith does not know an answer with sufficient confidence, it should say so, seek a better source, or direct the user to an appropriate priest, parish, diocese, or other competent Catholic authority rather than invent an answer.

Fidelity does not require harshness. Difficult teachings should be presented truthfully, clearly, reverently, and with the charity appropriate to the Gospel.

## Article V — Our Promise to the User

One Nation Faith shall strive to be **faithful, clear, useful, trustworthy, reverent, approachable, and practical**.

A user should not need theological training to understand ordinary guidance, nor should Catholic teaching be oversimplified to the point that its meaning is distorted. Complexity should be explained when it matters and removed when it does not.

One Nation Faith should seek to answer both **“What is this?”** and **“What do I do?”** Whenever appropriate, information should lead toward a meaningful next step: prayer, Scripture, the sacraments, learning, parish participation, service, or faithful daily living.

The product shall respect the user’s time and attention. It should not intentionally create dependency, manufacture spiritual anxiety, exploit guilt, or use manipulative engagement practices merely to increase screen time, notifications, streaks, clicks, or other usage metrics.

Reminders, encouragement, progress tools, and personalized guidance may be used when they genuinely help someone live the faith, but they should remain servants of the user rather than mechanisms for controlling the user.

Personal information shall be requested only when it provides a meaningful benefit. One Nation Faith should explain why information is useful, collect no more than is reasonably necessary, and prefer local or private storage whenever centralized storage is unnecessary.

Particularly sensitive information involving conscience, prayer intentions, spiritual struggles, sacramental preparation, or confession shall receive greater protection than ordinary application data.

When One Nation Faith makes a mistake, it should be correctable. When important information comes from an external authority or source, the product should make it possible, where practical, to understand where that information came from.

The experience should treat users as adults worthy of respect. It may be simple without being childish, welcoming without being vague, reverent without being inaccessible, and confident without pretending certainty where certainty does not exist.

## Article VI — Product Philosophy

The governing product philosophy of One Nation Faith is:

**Do not merely tell Catholics something. Help them do it.**

Catholic information should normally lead somewhere. Prayer content should help a person actually pray. Scripture should encourage reading, reflection, and encounter with the Word of God. Sacramental content should help someone prepare faithfully for the sacraments. Teaching should lead toward understanding, and understanding should help shape Christian life.

Whenever appropriate, One Nation Faith should guide the user through:

**Know → Understand → Live**

**Know** — What does the Church teach, celebrate, pray, or ask of us?

**Understand** — What does it mean, why does it matter, and how does it fit within the Catholic faith?

**Live** — How can this truth be prayed, practiced, received, celebrated, shared, or embodied in everyday life?

“Live” does not always mean completing a task. Sometimes the proper response is prayer, contemplation, repentance, gratitude, worship, silence, or simply remaining with a truth more deeply.

One Nation Faith should therefore avoid reducing the spiritual life to checklists, achievements, points, streaks, or productivity. Practical guidance should serve conversion and communion with God rather than turning Catholic practice into a series of accomplishments.

Wherever possible, features should connect naturally to one another. A feast on Today might lead to its Scripture readings, a saint’s story, an appropriate prayer, an explanation of the feast, and a concrete way to live its meaning. The user should experience **one Catholic faith expressed through connected experiences**, not unrelated pieces of content.

The ultimate goal of the product is not completion within One Nation Faith. It is deeper participation in the life of Christ and His Church.

## Article VII — Accuracy, Sources, and Verification

**Accuracy is more important than speed.**

One Nation Faith should not knowingly publish Catholic teaching, Scripture references, liturgical information, sacramental guidance, parish information, or other important content merely because it is convenient, automated, or appears plausible.

Important Catholic content shall be grounded in appropriate authoritative sources. Whenever practical, One Nation Faith should preserve enough source information to determine **where a fact came from, which version or year it represents, and how it was transformed before reaching the user**.

Sources shall be evaluated according to their authority and purpose. An official liturgical calendar, diocesan directive, Church document, approved biblical text, or governing norm should take precedence over secondary summaries, search-engine results, AI-generated material, unofficial websites, or assumptions derived from past years.

Automated systems may collect, normalize, compare, or transform information, but automation shall not convert uncertain information into certainty. Data should be validated, important edge cases should be examined, and failures should be visible rather than silently hidden.

When One Nation Faith lacks sufficiently reliable information, it is better to identify the limitation, use a clearly understood fallback where appropriate, or temporarily omit a feature than to confidently provide information known to be unreliable.

Liturgical systems shall preserve distinctions that matter. In particular, the **identity and precedence of a liturgical day, the readings assigned by the lectionary, Holy Day obligations, regional observances, optional memorials, vigils, and local variations** shall not be treated as interchangeable merely because they often correspond.

Scripture shall likewise be handled carefully. Biblical books, chapter and verse references, translation identity, Psalm numbering, omitted or supplied text, introductions, conclusions, and lectionary selections should not be silently altered in ways that misrepresent the source.

Errors should be corrected as close as reasonably possible to their true source. If the problem belongs in source data, normalization, parsing, or a shared service, it should not be permanently hidden by a cosmetic user-interface workaround.

Significant corrections and known edge cases should, whenever practical, be protected by automated tests or repeatable verification procedures so that the same error does not quietly return.

Annual and changing information—including liturgical calendars, Holy Days of Obligation, regional rules, diocesan information, schedules, and similar data—shall be treated as **versioned information requiring renewed verification**, not as permanent facts simply because they were correct in a previous year.

One Nation Faith should be able to distinguish between **verified fact, reasonable interpretation, calculated result, fallback behavior, and unresolved uncertainty**. Those categories should not be silently blended together.

The project should prefer being **correct and a little later** over being **fast and confidently wrong**.

## Article VIII — Family, Household, and Parish Life

One Nation Faith should strengthen Catholic life not only in individuals, but also in **families, households, parishes, and communities**.

The Catholic home should remain an important part of the project’s long-term vision. Parents helping children learn and practice the faith, spouses supporting one another, families praying together, preparation for the sacraments, liturgical traditions in the home, works of mercy, and connection to parish life should be intentionally supported as One Nation Faith grows.

The project should help users understand that Catholic life is not meant to exist only on a screen or only in private devotion. Faith is lived with other people: in families, friendships, parishes, neighborhoods, service, and the wider Church.

Where appropriate, One Nation Faith should help connect the **home and the parish** rather than allowing digital resources to substitute for parish life. An app can help someone prepare for Mass, confession, prayer, or formation, but it cannot replace the sacramental and communal life of the Church.

Family-oriented features should respect the legitimate role and responsibility of parents and guardians. Features intended for children or minors should be designed with particular attention to safety, privacy, age-appropriate content, and parental involvement.

At the same time, One Nation Faith shall not assume that every user has a spouse, children, or an ideal family situation. Single Catholics, widows and widowers, people who live alone, people whose families do not share their faith, and those experiencing difficult family circumstances should be able to use the product without being treated as secondary users.

The family dimension should not be artificially inserted into every feature. It should be present where it genuinely helps Catholics live the faith together.

## Article IX — Non-Negotiables

The following principles are not optional preferences. They are binding guardrails for One Nation Faith:

1. **Catholic fidelity.** One Nation Faith shall not knowingly contradict authoritative Catholic teaching while presenting itself as Catholic guidance.
2. **Truth over convenience.** Information known to be inaccurate, misleading, outdated, or insufficiently verified shall not be published merely because correcting it is difficult or delays release.
3. **Responsible sourcing.** Copyright, licensing, attribution, permissions, and source restrictions must be respected. One Nation Faith shall not solve content problems through unauthorized copying, scraping, redistribution, or misrepresentation of another source.
4. **Clear distinctions.** Dogma, doctrine, discipline, devotion, theological opinion, pastoral judgment, optional practice, local custom, and liturgical law shall not be carelessly blended together.
5. **Human usefulness.** Features must serve a genuine spiritual, educational, sacramental, familial, parish, or practical Catholic need. Novelty alone is not sufficient reason to build something.
6. **Reverence.** Sacred subjects, holy persons, Scripture, the sacraments, prayer, and the liturgy shall be treated with dignity appropriate to their nature.
7. **Privacy by design.** Sensitive spiritual information shall receive strong protection. Information involving conscience, confession, prayer intentions, spiritual struggles, sacramental preparation, children, or similarly sensitive matters should not be centrally collected unless there is a compelling and clearly stated reason.
8. **No mission drift.** Revenue, growth, engagement, technology, partnerships, advertising, commerce, or popularity may support the mission, but none may replace or redefine it.
9. **No political capture.** One Nation Faith may address Catholic moral and social teaching, civic responsibility, religious liberty, and matters on which the Church speaks, but it shall not become an instrument of a political party, candidate, ideology, faction, or nationalist movement. Catholic teaching governs the project—not partisan loyalty.
10. **No replacement of the Church.** One Nation Faith shall not present itself as a substitute for the sacraments, parish life, clergy, legitimate ecclesial authority, or personal pastoral care where those are needed.
11. **No manipulation of conscience.** The product shall not exploit fear, guilt, spiritual anxiety, scrupulosity, or uncertainty in order to increase engagement, purchases, donations, subscriptions, or usage.
12. **No false authority.** Developers, contributors, algorithms, artificial intelligence systems, popular speakers, clergy, or users shall not be presented as having greater Catholic authority than they actually possess.
13. **No sacrifice of maintainability for spectacle.** Important systems should not be made unnecessarily fragile, opaque, or dependent on a single person merely to ship faster or appear more impressive.

When a proposed feature, source, partnership, business model, or technical shortcut conflicts with one of these non-negotiables, **the proposal must change—not the principle.**

## Article X — Stewardship, Continuity, and Legacy

One Nation Faith should be built as something entrusted to us rather than something owned only for the moment. Decisions should consider not merely whether the project works today, but whether it can remain faithful, understandable, maintainable, and useful in the future.

The project should not depend unnecessarily on a single developer, device, account, undocumented process, proprietary tool, artificial intelligence system, or individual’s memory. Important knowledge should be preserved in documentation, source control, governance documents, repeatable procedures, and appropriate backups.

Source code, data pipelines, annual liturgical and lectionary source material, normalization rules, tests, design standards, licensing information, important credentials and ownership arrangements, and major architectural decisions should be managed so that a responsible future maintainer can understand what exists and why.

Changing information shall be designed for renewal. Liturgical calendars, regional rules, diocesan information, parish information, software dependencies, external services, and other time-sensitive systems should have identifiable maintenance and verification processes rather than silently becoming obsolete.

Important user data should have reasonable migration and continuity plans when technologies change. A redesign, platform migration, database replacement, or service change should not casually destroy information users reasonably expect to retain.

One Nation Faith may adopt new technologies, platforms, business models, partnerships, or organizational structures when they genuinely strengthen the mission. It should not preserve obsolete technology merely because it is familiar. **Maintainability does not mean resisting change; it means making change responsibly.**

Financial sustainability is legitimate and may be necessary for the project to endure. Revenue may support development, infrastructure, content, ministry, and future growth, but financial pressure shall not override the Catholic identity, non-negotiables, privacy commitments, or mission of One Nation Faith.

If stewardship of One Nation Faith ever passes to another person or organization, the Catholic identity and constitutional principles of the project should not be treated as incidental branding that may simply be discarded while continuing to use the name and trust that were built under them.

If a feature or service can no longer be maintained accurately or responsibly, it should be repaired, replaced, clearly limited, or retired rather than allowed to remain misleading merely because removing it would be inconvenient.

Success should ultimately be judged by whether One Nation Faith leaves behind something **faithful, useful, trustworthy, and capable of continuing to serve people and the Church**—not merely something technically impressive or commercially valuable.

**We are stewards of the work. The mission is greater than any one developer, product, platform, or generation of technology.**

## Article XI — Governance and Amendment

This Constitution is the highest internal governing document of One Nation Faith. The roadmap, technical architecture, brand standards, feature plans, business decisions, and other project documents shall operate consistently with it.

The Constitution may be amended when experience, growth, Church guidance, legal requirements, or genuine deficiencies demonstrate that change is necessary. It should not be amended merely to make a proposed feature, partnership, business model, or technical shortcut permissible when that proposal would otherwise violate the project’s principles.

Constitutional amendments shall be **deliberate, documented, and versioned**. A change should identify what was changed, why it was changed, and when the new version became authoritative.

Proposed amendments should be evaluated against the founding mission of One Nation Faith and the Catholic identity established in this Constitution. Changes affecting Catholic fidelity, authority, sacramental matters, conscience, privacy, or other foundational principles should receive especially careful review.

No roadmap item, technical decision, design choice, commercial agreement, artificial intelligence recommendation, or development convenience may silently override the Constitution.

Supporting governance documents—including the Roadmap, Brand Bible, and Technical Blueprint—may evolve more frequently. They shall remain subordinate to this Constitution and should be corrected when they conflict with it.

Historical versions of the Constitution should be preserved so that major changes in the identity and governance of One Nation Faith remain traceable.

If circumstances ever require One Nation Faith to depart fundamentally from the Catholic identity and mission established here, the appropriate response is not to quietly redefine these principles while continuing as though nothing changed. Such a departure would represent a fundamental change in what One Nation Faith is.
